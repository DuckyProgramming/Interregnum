Here's my current system for how the diet would work:

The king has the sole power to convene the diet but is legally required to do so every few years. The magnates can petition him informally for a diet, but he isn't forced to. The diet can be convened anywhere, though it usually remains in the major ecclesiastical and political cities. The king also sets the agenda, though some important matters are automatically discussed, and breaks ties if they arise. If the king is unavailable, the count palatine (in his capacity as Arch-Steward) represents him and assumes these powers. The king is allowed to vote if he holds any existing seats (such as him being a count).

Each of the three duchies or regions usually (but isn't required to) holds a pre-consultation before regular diet meetings where benches are coordinated and informal agreements might be made.

Women are able to hold diet seats, both temporal and ecclesiastical, but their prevalence varies by region.

The diet performs the following functions: confirming and establishing laws and privileges, arbitrating feuds and disagreements, funding and recruiting armies during extraordinary circumstances, and deciding on succession of extinct houses and regency when necessary.

There are a total of 50 votes. 40 votes are connected to territories:
[25] All counties (including the county palatine), landgraviates, and marquisates have their own seats.
[7] All archbishops have their own seats, except for that of Besançon (who holds only very smalll temporal lands but still gets an additional vote as an elector).
[2] All bishops who are also counts (Viviers, Basel) have their own seats.
[2] Free royal cities are organized into the following benches with one collective vote each:
Transjurania (Basel, Rheinfelden, Solothurn, Bern, Fribourg, Bremgarten, Lucerne),
Rhône (Geneva, Lyon, Besançon)
The Village Concord of Mirabel shares one collective position on the Rhône bench.
[4] The advocates, officers representing various states, appointed by the king but beholden to constituents, have a vote each:
Advocate of the northern lordships (minor lordships and baronies in the north)
Advocate of the southern lordships (minor lordships and baronies in the south)
Advocate of the royal abbies (royally immediate abbeys)
Advocate of the royal vallies (the free peasant states).
(For collective votes, only one member must appear in person to represent the others)

10 votes are connected to titles:
[7] The electors each have an additional vote.
[3] The ducal titles each grant an additional vote.

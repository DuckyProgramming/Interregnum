The king has the sole power to convene the diet but is legally required to do so every few years. The magnates can petition him informally for a diet, but he isn't forced to. The diet can be convened anywhere, though it usually remains in the major ecclesiastical and political cities. The king also sets the agenda and breaks ties if they arise. If the king is unavailable, the count palatine represents him and assumes these powers. The king is allowed to vote if he holds any existing seats (such as him being a count).

The Princess of Commercy attends but does not have a vote. Rather, she possesses the office of Mistress of the Royal Seal (Cancellaria Sigillum Regium), responsible for confirming the validity of and presenting documents. This position was originally part of the king's role but was ceded to Commercy. For political neutrality, the princess does not have a vote in the diet.

The diet performs the following functions: confirming and establishing laws and privileges, arbitrating feuds and disagreements, funding and recruiting armies during extraordinary circumstances, and deciding on succession of extinct houses and regency when necessary.
